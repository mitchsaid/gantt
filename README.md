# Gantt

A simple Gantt / project-plan chart maker built for charts you put on slides, proposals and reports.

No build step and no backend: it is a static site. Open `index.html`, or serve the folder:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

## What it does

- **Up to three levels** — areas, tasks and subtasks, plus milestones (kickoff, demo, go-live…). Areas are optional: a plan can be a flat list of tasks.
- **Direct editing** — drag bars to move them, drag their ends to resize, drag the progress edge, drag rows to reorder (in the outline or on the chart labels). Drag a bar mostly up or down on the chart to move it between rows and into existing areas — a drag never creates a new area (mostly sideways moves it in time). Drag a row sideways to nest it: an area dropped under another becomes a task and its tasks become subtasks. Click a bar for its name, dates, duration, owner and colour (in days, weeks, sprints or months); click the title or double-click a label to rename it in place.
- **Add from the chart** — the + Task / + Milestone / + Area buttons under the chart add after the selected row and let you name the new item in place.
- **Sensible new tasks** — a new task follows straight on from the previous one, and its default length scales with the timeline (about a fifth of it): a day or two on a short timeline, weeks on a year-long one, never most of it.
- **Timeline first** — click the date header (or use Design → Timeline) to fix the overall span, then spread the tasks across it, or pull in just the items that sit outside it (a notice appears under the chart when any do). An undated list pasted onto a fixed timeline is laid out to fill it.
- **Presentation options** — task names on the left or on the bars; area names as heading rows or down the side spanning their tasks; optional title; days, weeks, sprints (1–4 weeks), months or quarters; calendar dates or numbered periods ("Week 1"); dates at the top, bottom or both.
- **Rows** — one row per task, or "in sequence": tasks that follow one another within an area share a row, with names on the bars. Drag a task to the lower part of a row, or just under the last one, to put it on a line of its own; drag it onto a row to make it follow the tasks there.
- **Select, copy and paste** — Shift-click selects a range, Ctrl/⌘-click adds or removes items (on the chart or in the Plan panel). With one item selected, just start typing to rename it (F2 edits the existing name). Press Alt (⌥ on a Mac) + A, T or M to make the selection an area, task or milestone (or use the switch in an item's popover). An item's popover keeps its name, type and dates up top and tucks everything else (progress, assignees, dependencies, changes from the original, colour) into collapsible sections that show their current value and remember whether you left them open, with Duplicate and Delete always visible at the bottom. Press ↑ / ↓ to move the selection through the rows (Shift + ↑ / ↓ extends it to a range, and an open popover follows you). Flag an item as blocked from its popover (or press Alt/⌥ + B): its bar is striped with a red “!”, anything unfinished that depends on it gets lighter stripes, and the legend lists the reasons (Design → Status → Blocked items). For executive views: every item (and the plan, beside the title) can carry a green / amber / red status, areas roll up the worst of what is inside them; “Highlight the critical path” traces the dependency chain that decides the finish; with a baseline, the title shows the finish date and how many days it is later or earlier, and the legend says how far each explained change moved; a Key milestones strip sits above the chart on the same time scale; and non-working days (holidays, freezes) are shaded and left out of the working-day count in the item popover. “Reset to defaults…” at the bottom of the Design panel puts every design choice back (keeping your title and plan), or resets the plan too; Undo brings it back. With several items selected, Enter wraps them in a new area (Shift+Enter adds a line after them instead). Press Enter on a selected item to add a new one under it, or Shift+Enter to add a subtask inside it — a top-level task can hold subtasks without becoming an area (use the Area button in its details to make it one). A bar appears when several are selected with **Same length** (every selected task gets the average length) and **Add to new area** (Ctrl/⌘+G). Copy, cut, paste and duplicate (Ctrl/⌘ + C, X, V, D) work on the selection with everything inside it; drag one selected bar to move them all — sideways in time, or up and down into other areas. Pasted lists can be indented, bulleted, numbered, comma- or semicolon-separated (on one line or several), `Phase: a, b, c`, `Area > Task > Subtask`, or spreadsheet rows, and dates inside names (“Brochure 3 Mar – 14 Mar”) are picked up. Copied items are plain indented text, so they paste into a doc or spreadsheet and back again.
- **Add several at once** — paste a multi-line list straight into any name in the Plan panel, or use **Paste a list of tasks** in the Plan panel. Indentation, bullets, dates, durations and owners are understood, and undated items carry on after the selected row.
- **Item types** — switch any item between area, task and milestone; deleting an area or a task with subtasks asks whether to keep what is inside.
- **Optional layers** — dependency lines (rounded connectors in the colour of the task they follow), assignees (several per item, added as chips; under or beside the bars, or as round avatars on the bar with the names optionally to the right; for areas, tasks, subtasks or any mix), assignees can be cleared with one click; progress inside bars (Off, set per bar, or “up to the date marker” so bars fill up to it; a “days left for the whole plan” pill can sit beside the marker’s label, or at the top right if there is no marker; or switched off for a single bar, with an offer to switch it off for the whole chart once no bar uses it; shown as a pale unfinished part or a darker finished part), changes from the original plan (set a baseline; moved or lengthened items show their original dates under the bar, tinted in their own colour, with numbered reasons explained in a legend), an optional “N days left” annotation counted from the date marker (optionally weekdays only, with your own non-working days such as holidays or freezes left out), a draggable date marker (a line with an optional label — solid, dashed or dotted, in any thickness or colour), milestone guide lines.
- **Styling** — colour palettes, shared by area or by row so tasks on the same line match (bars take the palette in order and milestones all use its last colour; pick a ready-made one (eight categorical palettes whose colours are all clearly different hues, including a colour-blind-safe set), paste a single colour and choose from matching palettes, or build your own — start from any palette, edit, add (up to 16), reorder or remove colours, and save it by name for reuse — every colour is built to keep labels equally readable; recolour any one area, task or milestone from its details — from the palette, or any colour you like, with its label colour adjusted to stay readable), background colour or transparent, Google Fonts with automatic or exact text size (in points), wrapping long names onto two lines and a choice of what happens if they still don't fit (move outside, shrink, truncate), corner style, row height (Auto fills the canvas — short plans get taller bars — with Compact and Roomy as steps either side of it), separate period and month gridlines, stripes, and a canvas — a slide (16:9, 4:3), A4 or Letter page, square or custom size — that exports at exactly that size, with the height either following the plan or fixed.
- **Import** — paste an outline (tabs, spaces, bullets, numbering, Markdown headings, CSV/TSV, rows copied from a spreadsheet) or upload `.xlsx` / `.csv`. Dates, durations, owners and percentages are detected; undated lists are laid out as a waterfall.
- **Exec view** — one switch (above the chart, or Design → Canvas → View) redraws the plan as one bar per area, with its dates, progress, status and baseline rolled up, and the milestones in a strip above (the key ones, if any are marked). The plan itself is untouched and every export follows the view.
- **Automatic headline** — Design → Canvas → "Write the headline for me" writes the title from the plan: where the finish stands against the baseline or the overall status, then the biggest issue or the next milestone ("Go-live slips 2 weeks to 15 Jan; Build 2 weeks behind baseline"). Type over it on the chart to take it back.
- **Share link** — "Share link" copies a link that carries the whole plan and its design in the address itself (compressed, after the `#`), so nothing is uploaded. Opening it in a browser that already holds a different plan asks first. A link is a snapshot: later edits need a new link, and very large plans are better sent as a project file.
- **Export** — PowerPoint (native, editable shapes), PDF, PNG (3×), copy image to clipboard, and a `.json` project file.
- Zoom with Ctrl/⌘ + scroll or a trackpad pinch; both side panels collapse and can be resized (the widths are remembered).
- **Design panel in eight sections, with search** — Canvas (exec view, size, title), Timeline (periods, span, dates with a choice of formats such as 9 Dec, 9/12 or 9/12/26 in day- or month-first order, full or abbreviated month names, and the year on every month, never, or only where a new year begins, gridlines, weekends and your own non-working days), Tasks (row layout and height, bar corners, where names sit, assignees, dependencies and the critical path), Milestones (placement, guide lines, the key milestones strip), Progress (the date marker and its look, progress in bars, days left), Status (green / amber / red dots, blocked items, changes from the baseline), Colour (palette, background, text colour) and Text (font, sizes, name case and how long names fit). Each section collapses to a one-line summary, and which are open is remembered. The search box at the top (Ctrl/⌘ + K) narrows the panel to matching settings as you type, across names, options and everyday words (“holiday”, “owner”, “RAG”, “color”); a match that is hidden right now points to the switch that reveals it. Enter jumps into the first result, Esc clears.
- Autosaves to the browser, with undo/redo.

## Layout

- `js/core.js` — dates, colour, import parsing, and the chart layout (`buildScene`), which produces a renderer-independent list of shapes.
- `js/app.js` — state, the SVG/canvas/PowerPoint renderers, interactions and panels.
- `vendor/` — PptxGenJS, jsPDF and SheetJS, loaded only when an export or sheet import needs them.
