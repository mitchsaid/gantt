# Slideline

A simple Gantt / project-plan chart maker built for charts you put on slides, proposals and reports.

No build step and no backend: it is a static site. Open `index.html`, or serve the folder:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

## What it does

- **Up to three levels** — areas, tasks and subtasks, plus milestones (kickoff, demo, go-live…). Areas are optional: a plan can be a flat list of tasks.
- **Direct editing** — drag bars to move them, drag their ends to resize, drag the progress edge, drag rows to reorder (in the outline or on the chart labels). Drag a bar mostly up or down on the chart to move it between rows and into existing areas — a drag never creates a new area (mostly sideways moves it in time). Drag a row sideways to nest it: an area dropped under another becomes a task and its tasks become subtasks. Click a bar for its name, dates, duration, owner and colour (in days, weeks, sprints or months); click the title or double-click a label to rename it in place. While the title is being edited, a small bar under it offers **Write it for me** (the headline written from the plan), its size (S / M / L) and **Hide**; Esc puts the words back.
- **Clear hover and selection** — pointing at an item rings it on the chart (and marks its row in the Plan panel, and the other way round), with grips on the ends of bars that can be dragged to change their dates; a selected item gets a solid ring, and a single selected bar keeps its grips on the ring's ends, with a round knob on its lower edge for dragging progress. Grips and knob keep the same size on screen at any zoom, and their grab areas reach a little past the bar. The date header and title light up and say what a click does; the date marker is a control of its own — pointing at it thickens its line and puts drag arrows either side of its label.
- **Add from the chart** — the + Area / + Task / + Milestone buttons under the chart (dashed: they make something new) add after the selected row and let you name the new item in place. To their right, one toolbar holds the view switches, each saying what a click does: **Show / Exit exec view**, **Show / Hide progress**, **Set / Show / Hide baseline** (with the number of changed items on its corner, and ⋯ to update or remove it), and **Spread tasks** when the timeline is fixed. Each button keeps its width whatever it says, and notes such as “Adds after …” take a line of their own underneath, so nothing moves as things change.
- **Sensible new tasks** — a new task follows straight on from the previous one, and its default length scales with the timeline (about a fifth of it): a day or two on a short timeline, weeks on a year-long one, never most of it.
- **Timeline first** — the plan's overall span and length sit at the start of the toolbar above the chart (“Timeline Oct 26 – Jan 8 · 11 weeks”), always in the same place; click it or the date header (or use Design → Timeline) to fix the overall span, then spread the tasks across it, or pull in just the items that sit outside it (a notice appears under the chart when any do). An undated list pasted onto a fixed timeline is laid out to fill it.
- **Presentation options** — task names on the left or on the bars (aligned left, centred or right, optionally with each task's dates or duration under its name, in a slightly softer shade of the label colour, or after the bar on one line so the bars stay slim); area names as heading rows or down the side spanning their tasks; optional title; days, weeks, sprints (1–4 weeks), months or quarters; calendar dates or numbered periods ("Week 1"); dates at the top, bottom or both.
- **Rows** — one row per task, or "in sequence": tasks that follow one another within an area share a row, with names on the bars. Drag a task to the lower part of a row, or just under the last one, to put it on a line of its own; drag it onto a row to make it follow the tasks there.
- **Select, copy and paste** — Shift-click selects a range, Ctrl/⌘-click adds or removes items (on the chart or in the Plan panel). With one item selected, just start typing to rename it (F2 edits the existing name). Press Alt (⌥ on a Mac) + A, T or M to make the selection an area, task or milestone (or use the switch in an item's popover). An item's popover keeps its name, type and dates up top, then stacked rows: Status (a short menu) and Assignees always, and only what that item uses after them — progress, dependencies, a blocker, a note, the change from the baseline, key milestone, position in its day — added from one **+ Add** button. Rows with more to edit open their own page with a way back (Esc goes back too); Duplicate and Delete stay at the bottom. Press ↑ / ↓ to move the selection through the rows (Shift + ↑ / ↓ extends it to a range, and an open popover follows you). Give an item a blocker from its popover (+ Add → Blocker, or press Alt/⌥ + B): its bar is striped with a red “!”, anything unfinished that depends on it gets lighter stripes, and the legend lists the reasons (Design → Status → Blocked items). For executive views: every item (and the plan, beside the title) can carry a status, and areas roll up the worst of what is inside them. Statuses belong to the plan: the default On track / At risk / Off track can be renamed, recoloured, reordered from best to worst, added to or removed from any item's Status menu (Edit statuses…). A status named “Blocked” gets a nudge toward a Blocker, which keeps its reason. Any task or milestone can carry a one-line **note**: it gets a number beside its bar and a line in the legend, numbered together with the reasons for baseline changes (or keep it private); “Highlight the critical path” traces the dependency chain that decides the finish; with a baseline, the title shows the finish date and how many days it is later or earlier, and the legend says how far each explained change moved; a Key milestones strip sits above the chart on the same time scale; and non-working days (holidays, freezes) are shaded and left out of the working-day count in the item popover. “Reset to defaults…” at the bottom of the Design panel puts every design choice back (keeping your title and plan), or resets the plan too; Undo brings it back. With several items selected, Enter wraps them in a new area (Shift+Enter adds a line after them instead). Press Enter on a selected item to add a new one under it, or Shift+Enter to add a subtask inside it (or use **+ Subtask** and **+ Task below** at the foot of its popover and in its details in the Plan panel; an area's popover has **+ Task here**) — a top-level task can hold subtasks without becoming an area (use the Area button in its details to make it one). To select several without a keyboard, press **Select** at the top of the Plan panel, or **Select more** at the foot of an item's popover (it keeps that item selected and the bar says “1 selected — click more to add”): clicks or taps on the chart and on Plan rows then add or remove items (ticks show beside the rows), and **Done** or Esc ends it. A bar appears when several are selected with **Same length** (every selected task gets the average length) and **Add to new area** (Ctrl/⌘+G), plus Duplicate and Delete; in select mode the same bar sits at the top of the Plan panel. Copy, cut, paste and duplicate (Ctrl/⌘ + C, X, V, D) work on the selection with everything inside it; drag one selected bar to move them all — sideways in time, or up and down into other areas. Pasted lists can be indented, bulleted, numbered, comma- or semicolon-separated (on one line or several), `Phase: a, b, c`, `Area > Task > Subtask`, or spreadsheet rows, and dates inside names (“Brochure 3 Mar – 14 Mar”) are picked up. Copied items are plain indented text, so they paste into a doc or spreadsheet and back again.
- **Add several at once** — paste a multi-line list straight into any name in the Plan panel, or use **Paste a list of tasks** (under the Plan panel's add buttons, or, on a phone, **Paste a list…** after + Area / + Task / + Milestone under the chart). A **Shortcuts & tips** list sits tucked away at the foot of the Plan panel (closed unless you open it; hidden on touch screens). Indentation, bullets, dates, durations and owners are understood, and undated items carry on after the selected row.
- **Item types** — switch any item between area, task and milestone; deleting an area or a task with subtasks asks whether to keep what is inside.
- **Optional layers** — dependency lines (rounded connectors in the colour of the task they follow), assignees (several per item, added as chips; under or beside the bars, or as round avatars on the bar with the names optionally to the right; for areas, tasks, subtasks or any mix), assignees can be cleared with one click; progress inside bars (Off, set per bar, or “up to the date marker” so bars fill up to it; a “days left for the whole plan” pill can sit beside the marker’s label, or at the top right if there is no marker; or switched off for a single bar, with an offer to switch it off for the whole chart once no bar uses it; shown as a pale unfinished part or a darker finished part), changes from the original plan (set a baseline; moved or lengthened items show their original dates under the bar, tinted in their own colour, with numbered reasons explained in a legend), an optional “N days left” annotation counted from the date marker (optionally weekdays only, with your own non-working days such as holidays or freezes left out), a draggable date marker (a line with an optional label — solid, dashed or dotted, in any thickness or colour), milestone guide lines.
- **Look toolbar** — one row above the chart changes with what is selected. For a task: its own colour (“This task”), then for all tasks names on the left or on the bars, alignment, dates under names, text size and corners; for a milestone, guide lines and the key milestones strip; for an area, headings or side names and sharing colours; while the timeline is open, periods, where the dates sit and their format; with nothing selected, font, text size and palette. Pointing at a control (here, on the toolbar under the chart, on the title's options and on the Timeline pill) shows a quick tooltip with its name and what it does (“Pill-shaped bars — Changes every task”) and rings everything it will change on the chart (except the font and text size, which change all of it), “More” opens the full Design section, and pointing at a setting in the Design panel shows on the chart what it changes too.
- **Styling** — colour palettes, shared by area or by row so tasks on the same line match (bars take the palette in order and milestones all use its last colour; pick a ready-made one (eight categorical palettes whose colours are all clearly different hues, including a colour-blind-safe set), paste a single colour and choose from matching palettes, or build your own — start from any palette, edit, add (up to 16), reorder or remove colours, and save it by name for reuse — every colour is built to keep labels equally readable; recolour any one area, task or milestone from its details — from the palette, or any colour you like, with its label colour adjusted to stay readable), background colour or transparent, Google Fonts with automatic or exact text size (in points), wrapping long names onto two lines and a choice of what happens if they still don't fit (move outside, shrink, truncate), corner style, row height (Auto fills the canvas — short plans get taller bars — with Compact and Roomy as steps either side of it; on a fixed-height canvas, Auto and Compact tighten the rows, and automatic text down to about 8 pt, until a long plan fits, and warn only when even that isn't enough — the warning under the chart offers one-click fixes such as moving dates after the bars or letting the height follow the plan), separate period and month gridlines, stripes, and a canvas — a slide (16:9, 4:3), A4 or Letter page, square or custom size — that exports at exactly that size, with the height either following the plan or fixed.
- **Import** — paste an outline (tabs, spaces, bullets, numbering, Markdown headings, CSV/TSV, rows copied from a spreadsheet) or upload `.xlsx` / `.csv`. Dates, durations, owners and percentages are detected; undated lists are laid out as a waterfall.
- **Exec view** — one switch (above the chart, or Design → Canvas → View) redraws the plan as one bar per area, with its dates, progress, status and baseline rolled up, and the milestones in a strip above (the key ones, if any are marked). The plan itself is untouched and every export follows the view.
- **Automatic headline** — Design → Canvas → "Write the headline for me" writes the title from the plan: where the finish stands against the baseline or the overall status, then the biggest issue or the next milestone ("Go-live slips 2 weeks to 15 Jan; Build 2 weeks behind baseline"). Type over it on the chart to take it back.
- **Share link** — "Share link" copies a link that carries the whole plan and its design in the address itself (compressed, after the `#`), so nothing is uploaded. Opening it in a browser that already holds a different plan asks first. A link is a snapshot: later edits need a new link, and very large plans are better sent as a project file.
- **Plans from any AI** — `llms.txt` (served at https://slideline.co/llms.txt) tells ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity or a coding agent how to write a plan as a link: readable JSON with ISO dates, owners and dependencies by name, percent-encoded after `#plan=`. The AI needs web access to read the file. The link opens like a shared link (asks first if the browser holds another plan); a cut-off or invalid one says so. The Import dialog has a copyable prompt that points an AI at the file.
- **Export** — PowerPoint (native, editable shapes), PDF, PNG (3×), copy image to clipboard, and a `.json` project file.
- Zoom with Ctrl/⌘ + scroll or a trackpad pinch (two fingers on a phone or tablet); both side panels collapse and can be resized (the widths are remembered).
- **Design panel in eight sections, with search** — Canvas (exec view, size, title), Timeline (periods, span, dates with a choice of formats such as 9 Dec, 9/12 or 9/12/26 in day- or month-first order, full or abbreviated month names, and the year on every month, never, or only where a new year begins, gridlines, weekends and your own non-working days), Tasks (row layout and height, bar corners, where names sit, assignees, dependencies and the critical path), Milestones (placement, guide lines, the key milestones strip), Progress (the date marker and its look, progress in bars, days left), Status (green / amber / red dots, blocked items, changes from the baseline), Colour (palette, background, text colour) and Text (font, sizes, name case and how long names fit). Each section collapses to a one-line summary, and which are open is remembered. The search box at the top (Ctrl/⌘ + K) narrows the panel to matching settings as you type, across names, options and everyday words (“holiday”, “owner”, “RAG”, “color”); a match that is hidden right now points to the switch that reveals it. Enter jumps into the first result, Esc clears.
- **Phones and tablets** — the Plan and Design panels open as bottom sheets from the tab bar at the bottom of the screen. Tap or drag a sheet's handle up for full height, drag it down to shrink or close it; typing in a field (such as the settings search) gives the sheet the whole screen so the keyboard doesn't hide the results. Sheets span a phone's width and stay phone-sized and centred on a tablet; text fields use a 16px font so iOS doesn't zoom in, touch targets are larger, and controls that only appear on hover with a mouse are always shown on touch screens. Pinching the chart zooms the chart rather than the page (from the screen's width up), and a zoom control appears to reset it; a pinch never selects or drags what the fingers land on. A tap on a bar or a name selects it and opens its popover (so an area, too, can be deleted from the chart), and a selected bar can be dragged with one finger; its end grips and progress knob are bigger and easier to catch on a touch screen. Start and end dates in a popover sit side by side without overlapping. The look bar above the chart and the toolbar under it stay screen-wide and in view as the chart scrolls; swipe the look bar sideways for the rest of its controls.
- Autosaves to the browser, with undo/redo.

## Layout

- `js/core.js` — dates, colour, import parsing, and the chart layout (`buildScene`), which produces a renderer-independent list of shapes.
- `js/app.js` — state, the SVG/canvas/PowerPoint renderers, interactions and panels.
- `vendor/` — PptxGenJS, jsPDF and SheetJS, loaded only when an export or sheet import needs them.
